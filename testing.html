Who Gets Counted?

Intimate Partner Violence & Data

Binary Bros · DH 101

Project Statement

Intimate partner violence (IPV) includes physical violence, sexual violence, stalking, and psychological harm by a current or former partner. However, how data is collected, who is represented, and what researchers choose to measure regarding intimate partner violence can only shape our understanding. This project examines how global IPV data is gathered and modeled, what it can reveal about violence across populations, and which experiences may remain unseen.

Research Questions

How does intimate partner violence vary in prevalence and reporting across different cultural and socio-economic contexts? What factors influence these variations?

How has the rate of IPV fluctuated over the years? Could external global factors contribute to the percentage of women who experience IPV?

Historical Timeline (1962 - 2019)

Although there have been records of response to domestic violence dating back to the early 1500's, we will discuss signficant events dating from 1962 to 2019. Reading across the timeline, the expansion from local crisis lines and shelters in the 1970s, to court rulings and state laws in the 1980s and 1990s, to national legislation that is renewed over time.

data critique.

A dataset is a record of choices. Open each question below and answer it for your own datasets.

Who collected this, and why?
Name the agency or group and its purpose. A police record and a public health survey answer different questions.
Who is counted?
Note age range, relationship types, gender categories and who had to take part for a case to appear.
Who is missing?
Think about people who do not report, cannot access services, or fall outside the definitions used.
What could change the numbers?
Changes in law, funding, wording or outreach can raise or lower counts without any change in lived reality.
