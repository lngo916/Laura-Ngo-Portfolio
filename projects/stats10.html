Overview

We want to determine if there is a strong correlation between poverty and COVID-19 death rates.

A linear regression model can show the relationship between these variables by fitting a linear equation to the observed data.

Diagnostics

The residuals are the problem.

Residuals are what the line misses. Almost every county sits just under the fitted line while a few sit far above it.

Residual summary

Minimum, quartiles and maximum of the residuals, in deaths. The maximum is more than 60 times the size of any other value.

Factors to be concerned about

  • Strong skew. The median residual is −1.19 and the maximum is 102.9. Most counties report few deaths and a handful report very many.
  • Extreme observations. A few large counts dominate the residuals and can pull the fitted line.
  • Assumptions. Linear regression expects residuals that are roughly symmetric with constant spread. These are not.
  • What the model leaves out. Deaths are raw counts, and county population is not in the model. Larger counties will tend to record more deaths whatever their poverty rate.

Findings

A weak downward trend that the data cannot support.

The trend

Counties with higher poverty rates tend to report slightly fewer deaths. A 10-point rise in poverty (0.10) goes with about 0.28 fewer deaths a week. The correlation of −0.037 is very close to zero, and the slope's p-value of 0.0594 is just above the 5% threshold, so the trend is not statistically significant at that level.

What R² means here

R² is 0.00135. Differences in poverty rate account for about 0.13% of the differences in weekly deaths between counties. The other 99.87% comes from factors outside the model, such as county size, and from chance.

How far to trust it

The pattern describes an association in these counties, not a cause. The skewed residuals mean the standard errors and p-value rest on assumptions the data do not meet. A fuller analysis would adjust for population and check that a few extreme counties are not driving the result.
