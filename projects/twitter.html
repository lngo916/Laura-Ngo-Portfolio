01 · Research Motivation

Questions & Motivations

Three questions

What textual characteristics distinguish Donald Trump's flagged tweets from those that were not flagged by Twitter's moderation system between 2009 and 2021?
How accurately can natural language processing and machine learning models predict whether a tweet will be flagged based on its textual features?
How did the frequency and characteristics of flagged tweets change over time, particularly around the 2020 U.S. presidential election?

About this Project

This project examines the relationship between political communication and platform moderation policies, focusing on Donald Trump's tweets from 2009 to 2021 to predict which are likely to be flagged by Twitter's moderation system. Particularly surrounding the 2020 election, his collection of tweets provides a rich dataset for exploring textual characteristics, using natural language processing and machine learning techniques.

Dataset notes

  • Span: 2009 to 2021
  • Tweets: 46,694
  • Flagged: 250 (0.54%)

02 · Processing

Data Processing

01
Transform

Transform columns "isRetweet", "isDeleted", and "isFlagged" into boolean and remove retweets.

02
Remove

Remove stop words and stem/tokenize the text to reduce data dimensionality.

03
Text Processing

Conduct TfidVectorizer to transform text data (X) into numerical features, accounting for word importance based on recurrence.

04
Resample

Use RandomOverSampler to select random examples from the from the minority class with replacement, supplementing the training data with multiple copies of this instance (note: can increase likelihood of overfitting).

05
Reduction

Use TruncatedSVD to reduce dimensions through PCA, transforming our X.

06
Split

Split data into 80% training / 20% testing.

07
Assign

Use TextBlob to assign sentiment polarity scores to the individual tweets.
